## 🎒 Master Course Homework Catalog (15-Minute Daily Practical Tasks)

> **Core Instructional Rule for Homework:**  
> Every assignment takes **strictly 15 minutes or less**, requires **zero advanced coding**, uses **everyday tools (paper, notepad, smartphone)**, and directly prepares the learner for the very first hands-on activity of the next morning.

---

### 📝 Day 1 Homework: "The Smartphone Detective"
* **Target Lesson:** Day 1: Computer & Application Basics
* **Time Required:** 10 – 15 minutes.
* **Materials Needed:** Any personal smartphone, notepad or sticky note, pen.
* **Objective:** Train the brain to see the universal Input ➔ Process ➔ Output rhythm in everyday technology.
* **Step-by-Step Instructions:**
  1. Open any single app you use every day (e.g., Alarm Clock, Weather, WhatsApp, or Maps).
  2. Perform one quick action (set a 7:00 AM alarm, look up the weather forecast, or send a short greeting).
  3. On your notepad, write down 3 clear bullet points:
     - 📥 **Input:** What exact button or screen area did your thumb touch?
     - ⚙️ **Process:** What did the phone's internal processor do? (e.g., matched time, fetched forecast data, saved text).
     - 📤 **Output:** What came back to you? (e.g., a bell icon, a temperature number, a sent confirmation checkmark).
* **Deliverable to Bring to Class:** 1 sticky note with your 3 bullets to share with your pair-programming partner in Day 2's morning icebreaker.
* **Self-Check Question:** *"Can you explain where the Process happens without using confusing engineering jargon?"* (Answer: Inside the phone's chip, out of sight!)

---

### 📝 Day 2 Homework: "The Workplace Paper Form Hunter"
* **Target Lesson:** Day 2: Data, Tables & Labels
* **Time Required:** 12 – 15 minutes.
* **Materials Needed:** 1 physical paper form from your factory or office (or a supermarket/restaurant paper receipt), pen.
* **Objective:** Understand how real-world data types (Text, Number, Date, Yes/No) are organized before converting them into digital Power Apps fields.
* **Step-by-Step Instructions:**
  1. Pick up any paper document at work or home: an equipment maintenance log, a material requisition sheet, an overtime request, or a store receipt.
  2. Identify 5 distinct pieces of information written on that paper.
  3. Label each piece of data with its computer data type:
     - 🔤 **Text (Letters & Words):** e.g., Employee Name, Machine Name, Description.
     - 🔢 **Number (Math Values):** e.g., Quantity, Temperature, Total Cost, Hours.
     - 📅 **Date & Time:** e.g., Inspection Date, Shift Start Time.
     - 🔘 **Yes/No (Boolean Choice):** e.g., Passed Inspection (Yes/No), Urgent (Checked/Unchecked).
* **Deliverable to Bring to Class:** The paper form with your 5 data-type labels circled in pen.
* **Self-Check Question:** *"Why should an Employee Phone Number or ID be stored as Text instead of a Number?"* (Answer: Because you will never add or multiply phone numbers together!)

---

### 📝 Day 3 Homework: "The Paper Wireframe Architect"
* **Target Lesson:** Day 3: Canvas Studio & Visual Controls
* **Time Required:** 10 – 15 minutes.
* **Materials Needed:** 1 blank sheet of paper, pen or pencil.
* **Objective:** Build visual layout confidence by sketching a screen before touching Power Apps Canvas Studio.
* **Step-by-Step Instructions:**
  1. Draw a simple tall rectangle on your paper representing a mobile phone screen (or a wide rectangle for a tablet).
  2. At the top, sketch a **Header Bar** and write the title of an app you want to build (e.g., *"Shift Safety Check"*).
  3. In the middle, draw a **Card or List Box** showing 2 lines of sample information (e.g., Machine A12 — Status: Running).
  4. At the bottom, draw a **Big Action Button** and write what tapping it will do (e.g., *"+ Submit New Report"*).
* **Deliverable to Bring to Class:** Your hand-drawn paper screen sketch. We will turn this exact drawing into live Power Apps controls during Day 4!
* **Self-Check Question:** *"Is your button big enough to be easily tapped by a factory worker wearing gloves?"* (Answer: Yes, keep buttons tall and spacious!)

---

### 📝 Day 4 Homework: "The Workplace IF/THEN Rulebook"
* **Target Lesson:** Day 4: Power Fx Logic & Variables
* **Time Required:** 12 – 15 minutes.
* **Materials Needed:** Notepad, pen.
* **Objective:** Master conditional business logic in plain conversational English before translating it into Power Fx `If(...)` formulas.
* **Step-by-Step Instructions:**
  1. Think of two real rules or safety guidelines followed in your workplace.
  2. Write each rule strictly using the two magic words: **IF** and **THEN** (and optionally **ELSE**).
     - *Example 1 (Safety):* **IF** Machine Temperature > 80°C, **THEN** display a Red Warning Banner, **ELSE** display a Green "Normal" Badge.
     - *Example 2 (Support):* **IF** Request Priority is "Urgent", **THEN** send email to Department Manager, **ELSE** add to standard queue.
* **Deliverable to Bring to Class:** 2 written plain-English rules. In class, we will turn them directly into Power Fx: `If(Temp > 80, Color.Red, Color.Green)`.
* **Self-Check Question:** *"Does your rule have a clear True outcome and a clear False outcome?"* (Answer: Yes, every decision has two paths!)

---

### 📝 Day 5 Homework: "The 5-Column SharePoint Blueprint"
* **Target Lesson:** Day 5: SharePoint Lists & Data Storage
* **Time Required:** 12 – 15 minutes.
* **Materials Needed:** Notepad, ruler, pen.
* **Objective:** Design a clean, structured table schema before creating your cloud SharePoint List.
* **Step-by-Step Instructions:**
  1. On a clean page, draw a table with **5 columns**.
  2. Label the columns for a workplace "Equipment Issue & Support Log":
     - Column 1: `RequestID` (e.g., REQ-1001)
     - Column 2: `ItemName` (e.g., Conveyor Motor 3)
     - Column 3: `Requester` (e.g., Sokha Chan)
     - Column 4: `Priority` (e.g., High)
     - Column 5: `Status` (e.g., Pending)
  3. Write down **2 realistic rows of sample data** inside your table.
* **Deliverable to Bring to Class:** Your 5-column table design. We will create this exact list live in SharePoint within 5 minutes in Day 5!
* **Self-Check Question:** *"Did you include a column to track who requested the support?"* (Answer: Yes, every business app must track the Requester!)

---

### 📝 Day 6 Homework: "The 3-Step Human Workflow Map"
* **Target Lesson:** Day 6: Power Automate Approval Flows
* **Time Required:** 10 – 15 minutes.
* **Materials Needed:** Notepad, pen.
* **Objective:** Map out the exact human communication journey behind automated notifications and supervisor approvals.
* **Step-by-Step Instructions:**
  1. Draw 3 horizontal boxes connected by arrows: `[Step 1] ➔ [Step 2] ➔ [Step 3]`.
  2. Fill in the boxes for a workplace request approval:
     - **Step 1 (The Trigger):** Operator submits a new Support Request in Power Apps.
     - **Step 2 (The Decision):** Automated email/Teams notification is sent to Supervisor with [Approve] and [Reject] buttons.
     - **Step 3 (The Conclusion):** If approved, update status to "Approved" and alert the technician; if rejected, notify requester with comments.
* **Deliverable to Bring to Class:** Your 3-step workflow diagram. We will configure this automated flow directly in Power Automate in Day 6!
* **Self-Check Question:** *"What happens if the supervisor clicks Reject?"* (Answer: The app must record the rejection reason and alert the worker!)

---

### 📝 Day 7 Capstone Homework: "The 3-Minute Peer Showcase"
* **Target Lesson:** Day 7: End-to-End Enterprise Support App Launch
* **Time Required:** 15 minutes.
* **Materials Needed:** Your computer/tablet running the completed Support Request App, 1 coworker or friend.
* **Objective:** Validate real-world usability, build presentation confidence, and practice gathering user feedback.
* **Step-by-Step Instructions:**
  1. Sit down with 1 coworker, supervisor, or friend for 3 minutes.
  2. Ask them to pretend they need urgent maintenance on a factory machine.
  3. Hand them your tablet/laptop and observe silently without helping:
     - Can they find the "New Request" button?
     - Can they type their issue and select "Urgent"?
     - Does the confirmation banner make them feel confident?
  4. Ask them one single question: *"What is one thing that could make this app even easier for you?"*
  5. Write down their answer on a sticky note.
* **Deliverable to Bring to Graduation:** The feedback note and your self-reflection. Congratulations, you are now an official Power Apps Citizen Developer!
* **Self-Check Question:** *"Did your user successfully submit a request without needing you to explain how?"* (Answer: Yes, intuitive UI always wins!)

### 1. Measurable Learning Objectives
By the end of this 90-minute session, without consulting notes, the learner will be able to:
1. **Identify the Real Problem:** Given any workplace bottleneck, separate the human pain point (*the problem*) from the business outcome (*the result*) with 100% accuracy.
2. **Trace the Universal 5-Step Chain:** Correctly map any physical or digital interaction across `Problem ➔ Input ➔ Process ➔ Output ➔ Result` without using engineering jargon.
3. **Deconstruct an Everyday Machine:** Identify the exact Inputs, internal Process rules, and sensory Outputs of common devices (vending machine, microwave).
4. **Architect a Workplace Support Request:** Formulate the 2 required inputs and 1 primary output for a factory tablet kiosk to report machine breakdown.

### 3. Prerequisite Retrieval Questions (Baseline Check)
* **Question 1:** *"When you put a $1 coin into a drink vending machine and press button 'B-4', what are you doing?"*
  * **Expected Answer:** You are giving the machine resources (money) and instructions (choice B-4) so it knows what drink to drop.
* **Question 2:** *"Your coffee is cold. You place it in the microwave, dial 60 seconds, and press Start. What is the real problem?"*
  * **Expected Answer:** The problem is cold coffee. The microwave heats it (process), and the hot cup you drink is the result.

### 4. The "WHY THIS EXISTS" Problem (Workplace Pain)
* **The Paper Reality at Apex Plastics:** Machine #4 leaks hydraulic fluid at 08:15 AM. Operator writes note on clipboard. Oil spills on note. Message lost. Machine seizes at 02:15 PM causing 6 hours of downtime ($14,400 loss).
* **What an App Does Differently:** Operator taps "Machine #4", selects "Oil Leak", taps Submit on tablet. Machine logs exact second, alerts tech's smartphone. Technician arrives in 12 minutes with gaskets and fresh oil. Zero downtime!
* **Key Distinction:** The app did not repair the machine; the human mechanic did. But the app made the communication instant, permanent, and legible.

---

### 5. Plain-Language Concept Before Technical Vocabulary
* **The 1-Sentence Definition:** An application is a helpful, obedient digital assistant that accepts information, follows strict rules, and delivers a useful result.
* **Three Plain Truths:**
  1. *An app is never magical:* Every action is an instruction given by a human.
  2. *An app cannot think:* It cannot guess what you meant; it only runs what you entered.
  3. *An app exists for humans:* If it doesn't solve a real pain, it shouldn't exist.

### 13. 5-Question Knowledge Check (With Answers)
1. **Difference between Output and Result?** Output is digital data emitted by the machine; Result is the physical improvement in the real world.
2. **What happens if a user inputs wrong data?** The app strictly processes the wrong data and gives wrong outputs.
3. **Why replace paper with Power Apps?** Paper gets lost, damaged, delayed, and unreadable; digital data is instant and permanent.
4. **What is Process in our Kiosk?** Generating ticket ID, stamping time, and formatting the dispatch record.
5. **App shows "Success!" but mechanic never alerted?** The process failed to bridge to the useful real-world result.

#### 🎙️ Hardware vs. Software Script (Part 2: Minutes 08:00 – 20:00)
> *"Before we talk about apps, let's understand two basic words: **Hardware** and **Software**.*
>
> *Imagine a musical piano sitting in a room. The wooden frame, the steel strings, and the white and black keys are things you can touch with your hands. That is **Hardware**. A laptop, a tablet screen, a keyboard, and an RFID badge reader are all Hardware.*
>
> *Now, what good is a piano if nobody plays music? Beside the piano sits a book of sheet music with notes printed on paper. The sheet music tells the pianist which keys to strike and when. That sheet music is **Software**.*
>
> *An **Application**, or **App**, is simply a software book of instructions designed to do one specific job for a human being. A calculator app helps you calculate numbers. An attendance app helps you record your work hours. A camera app helps you take pictures.*
>
> *And who is the most important person in this entire room? The **User**! The user is the human being who uses the tool to do their job. When we build Power Apps, we never build them to look fancy; we build them to help Maria on Assembly Line 2 finish her shift safety check without stress!"*

#### 🎙️ The Golden Rule: IPO Script (Part 3: Minutes 20:00 – 38:00)
> *"Every computer in the world — from the pocket calculator in your pocket to supercomputers at NASA — obeys one single universal rhythm:*
>
> $$\text{INPUT} \longrightarrow \text{PROCESS} \longrightarrow \text{OUTPUT}$$
>
> *Let's look at our kitchen blender:*
> * 📥 **Input:** You chop up bananas, pour in milk, drop in ice cubes, and push the 'Blend' button.
> * ⚙️ **Process:** The electric motor spins the stainless steel blades at high speed, liquefying the fruit.
> * 📤 **Output:** A cool, delicious smoothie pours into your glass.
>
> *What happens if you forget the fruit and just push the button? The machine runs, but you get nothing to drink. What happens if you put rocks in the blender? You break the blades! Computers work the exact same way:*
> *If the user provides clean input, the computer runs clean processing, and you get a clean output!*"*

#### 🎙️ Introducing Power Fx Script (Minutes 32:00 – 38:00)
> *"In Microsoft Power Apps, we use a friendly formula language called **Power Fx**. It works just like formulas in Microsoft Excel.*
>
> *Here is our golden rule for Power Fx: **Speak the human sentence in plain English first!***
>
> *Listen to the human thought:*  
> *'Computer, take the first number 7, add it to the second number 3, and show me the total.'*
>
> *Now look at how Power Fx writes that thought:*  
> `7 + 3`
>
> *Notice:*
> * `7` and `3` are the **Input** values.
> * The `+` sign is the **Process** command telling Power Apps to combine them.
> * The label widget on your screen displaying `10` is the **Output**!
>
> *You see? You already understand formula logic before we have even opened the software!"*

### 8. Common Beginner Traps & Trainer Interventions

#### Trap 1: Confusing the Physical Screen with the Process
* **Learner Mistake:** *"The tablet screen is the processor because it shows the numbers."*
* **Trainer Intervention:** *"Remember the television at home. The TV screen doesn't write the movie; it only shows what the broadcast antenna receives. The screen is pure **Output**. The chip hidden inside the casing is doing the **Processing**!"*

#### Trap 2: The "Magic Leap" (Skipping the Process)
* **Learner Mistake:** *"I pushed the button and the door unlocked. Input leads directly to Output!"*
* **Trainer Intervention:** *"It feels instantaneous, but there is always a middle step. When you tapped your badge, the computer had to search 1,000 employees in memory to check if your badge was valid. That was the invisible **Process**."*

#### Trap 3: Expecting the Computer to "Know What I Meant"
* **Learner Mistake:** *"Why did the calculator give me an error when I typed 5 divided by 0?"*
* **Trainer Intervention:** *"Computers are obedient, but they have zero intuition. If you feed bad input, the computer will faithfully process bad input. In computer science, we call this **GIGO: Garbage In, Garbage Out**."*

#### Trap 4: Overcomplicating Power Fx Early
* **Learner Mistake:** *"Do I need to learn programming syntax like C++ or JavaScript to build this?"*
* **Trainer Intervention:** *"Zero! Look at how you do math on paper: `7 + 3`. In Power Apps, you write the exact same thing: `7 + 3`. Keep it simple!"*

>
> *To a robot or computer, you must give bite-sized commands: Walk to the counter. Pick up the pan. Turn on the burner. That is an instruction. Clear, direct, and unambiguous."*

#### 🎙️ The Law of Order: Socks Before Shoes (Part 3: Minutes 22:00 – 38:00)
> *"Now let's talk about **Sequence**. Sequence means: **The exact order in which things happen**.*
>
> *Think about getting dressed in the morning:*
> * *Instruction A: Put on socks.*
> * *Instruction B: Put on heavy work boots.*
>
> *If you do Instruction A first, and Instruction B second, you are ready for a safe day at work.*
>
> *Now, what happens if you reverse the sequence? You put on your boots first, and then try to stretch your wool socks over the outside of the boots! Can you do it? No! It ruins the socks, looks ridiculous, and protects nothing.*
>
> *Notice: Both instructions were good instructions! The failure happened purely because the **SEQUENCE** was wrong. Computers are the same way: if you tell an app to show a success message before saving the form, chaos follows!"*

### 7. Common Beginner Mistakes & Trainer Interventions

1. **Trap 1: The "Mind Reader" Fallacy**
   - *Mistake:* Assuming the computer knows to save data before changing screens.
   - *Intervention:* *"Remember: Computers have zero intuition. If you do not explicitly instruct it to save, it will forget everything."*
2. **Trap 2: Putting Shoes on First (Cause & Effect Swap)**
   - *Mistake:* Trying to display a success message before the form is validated or saved.
   - *Intervention:* *"Golden Rule: Always save first, THEN celebrate! Never celebrate an empty promise."*
3. **Trap 3: Vague Button Labels**
   - *Mistake:* Labeling action buttons with single vague words like *"Go"* or *"Click"*.
   - *Intervention:* *"Use strong verb-noun pairings: 'Submit Issue Report', 'Confirm Inspection', 'Clear Form'."*
4. **Trap 4: Missing the Finish Line (Feedback Vacuum)**
   - *Mistake:* Submitting an app form without showing a confirmation banner.
   - *Intervention:* *"Always close the loop! Give the operator a clear green banner or audio chime confirming their report was logged."*

### 1. 🎯 Measurable Learning Objectives
By the end of this 90-minute session, the absolute beginner learner will be able to:

2. **Master the 4 Comparison Symbols:** Fluently identify and explain:

3. **From Day 3 (Data Types):** *"What data type is ALWAYS produced when a computer evaluates a condition?"*

### 4. 🗣️ Trainer Speaking Notes & Script

#### Part 1: Welcome to the Logic Phase (00:00 – 10:00)
> *"Welcome to Day 4! Today we officially cross into Phase 2: LOGIC. Over the last 3 days, you learned what computers are and what data they hold. Today, you will give your computer a brain!*  
> *How does an app make decisions? How does it know when to turn an alert RED, when to lock a door, or when to notify a supervisor? It all comes down to two simple words: TRUE and FALSE.*  
> *If you have ever checked the weather outside and decided whether to wear a jacket, you are already an expert in logic. Today we simply teach your computer to do the same thing!"*

#### Part 2: Statements vs. Conditions (10:00 – 25:00)
> *"What is a statement? If I say 'The factory lights are on', you can look at the ceiling and say: 'Yes, True!' or 'No, False!' That is a statement.*  
> *If I say 'This machine is pretty', can a computer evaluate that? No! That is an opinion. Computers only deal with measurable reality.*  
> *In an app, a CONDITION is a statement written with comparison symbols: `RoomTemperature > 24`. If the room is 28, the verdict is TRUE, so the air conditioner blows cold air! When the room cools down to 22, the statement becomes FALSE, and the compressor sleeps."*

#### Part 3: The 4 Master Comparison Symbols (25:00 – 45:00)
> *"Let's meet our 4 symbols:  
> 1. **Equal To (`=`):** Asks 'Is left identical to right?' `\"Dara\" = \"Dara\"` is TRUE.*  
> 2. **Not Equal To (`<>`):** Two arrows pointing away from each other: 'They don't agree!' `10 <> 5` is TRUE.*  
> 3. **Greater Than (`>`):** The Hungry Alligator rule! The open mouth always points toward the bigger number: `100 > 20`.*  
> 4. **Less Than (`<`):** Tilts like a capital letter 'L' for LESS: `5 < 50`.*  
> *And what if you need to pass an exam where 50 is passing? If you write `Score > 50`, anyone who gets exactly 50 fails! To include the boundary number itself, we write `>=` (Greater than OR Equal to). Always put the alligator first, and the equal sign second: `>=` or `<=`!"*

#### Part 4: Hands-On Practice: 4 Workplace Cases (45:00 – 65:00)
> *"Look at our 4 manufacturing cases on screen:  
> - Case 1: Machine temp is 74°C. Condition: `74 > 80`. Result: FALSE! No alarm.  
> - Case 2: Stock is 8 bolts. Condition: `8 < 10`. Result: TRUE! Orange alert displays.  
> - Case 3: Logged in user is 'Operator'. Condition: `\"Operator\" = \"Inspector\"`. Result: FALSE! Lock the approval button.  
> - Case 4: Safety guard is closed (`true`). Condition: `true = true`. Result: TRUE! Motor is safe to start!"*

#### Part 5: Knowledge Check (65:00 – 75:00)

### 5. 💡 Simple Explanations & Everyday Analogies

### 8. ⚠️ Common Beginner Mistakes & Trainer Interventions

| Beginner Mistake | Why It Happens | Trainer Intervention & Fix |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Thinking `=` is an order** | In math/life, `=` assigns answers. In logic, `=` only ASKS: *"Is it equal?"* | Explain: *"In conditions, `=` is a question mark, not a command. It never changes the data."* |
| **Mixing up `<` and `>`** | Beginners confuse which side is bigger. | Use the **Hungry Alligator**: open mouth eats the bigger value; pointed tip points to the smaller value. |
| **Thinking `<>` is a diamond or broken code** | Beginners haven't seen `<>` on a typewriter. | Explain: *"Think of `<>` as two people turning their backs on each other — Not Equal!"* |
| **Forgetting quotes when comparing text** (`Status = Open`) | Beginners forget that words need quotes in code. | Remind them: *"Quotes are the speech bubble that tells the computer: 'This is a human word'!"* |
| **Forgetting boundary numbers** (`>` vs `>=`) | Writing `> 50` when wanting to include 50. | Show that `50 > 50` is False, but `50 >= 50` is True. Always add the equal sign! |

### 9. 🧠 5 Knowledge-Check Questions (With Answers & Explanations)

#### Q1: What is the result of the condition: `15 <> 15`?
* A) TRUE  
* B) FALSE *(Correct)*  
* C) 0  
* D) 'Error'  
* **Answer:** **B) FALSE.**  
* **Explanation:** 15 IS equal to 15. Therefore, claiming that 15 is NOT equal to 15 is a FALSE statement!

#### Q2: If an oven temperature is exactly 80°C, what does `Temperature >= 80` evaluate to?
* A) FALSE  
* B) TRUE *(Correct)*  
* C) 80  
* D) None  
* **Answer:** **B) TRUE.**  
* **Explanation:** Because the operator includes the equal sign (`>=`), 80 satisfies the boundary condition!

#### Q3: Which comparison symbol means "Not Equal To" in Microsoft Power Apps?
* A) `!=`  
* B) `<>` *(Correct)*  
* C) `==`  
* D) `><`  
* **Answer:** **B) `<>`.**  
* **Explanation:** Power Apps and Excel standard formula syntax uses `<>` to represent "Not Equal".

#### Q4: If an operator is logged in with Role = "Operator", what does `"Operator" = "Supervisor"` evaluate to?
* A) TRUE  
* B) FALSE *(Correct)*  
* C) "Operator"  
* D) Error  
* **Answer:** **B) FALSE.**  
* **Explanation:** The text strings "Operator" and "Supervisor" do not match character-for-character.

#### Q5: A warehouse inventory has 4 boxes left. Condition: `BoxesLeft < 5`. What is the result?
* A) TRUE *(Correct)*  
* B) FALSE  
* C) 4  
* D) 5  
* **Answer:** **A) TRUE.**  
* **Explanation:** 4 is strictly less than 5. The condition is TRUE, triggering the re-order alert!
