## 🎯 1. Four Measurable Learning Objectives (Bloom's Taxonomy)

By the end of this 90-minute session, the absolute beginner learner will independently demonstrate:

1. **Objective 1 (Diagnosis & Analysis - Bloom Level 4):**  
   Distinguish between a genuine **Power Apps Opportunity** and a non-app issue (e.g. policy change, static bulletin, basic email, physical thermostat) across 6 industrial scenarios with 100% accuracy using the *5-Part Opportunity Canvas*.
2. **Objective 2 (Stakeholder & Information Scoping - Bloom Level 2):**  
   Identify and map the three foundational roles (**Requester**, **Actor/Worker**, **Supervisor/Viewer**) and their mandatory data fields for any workplace operational bottleneck.
3. **Objective 3 (Business Rule Extraction - Bloom Level 3):**  
   Convert vague human workplace requests (e.g., *"We need urgent maintenance handled faster"*) into concrete, structured business rules ready for logic gates: `Condition (If) ➔ Action (Then) ➔ Fallback (Else)`.
4. **Objective 4 (Opportunity Blueprint Formulation - Bloom Level 5):**  
   Author a complete 1-page *App Opportunity Blueprint* for the Apex Plastics Tooling Die Maintenance bottleneck, specifying Current Pain, Desired Result, Users, Captured Data, and Decision Rules.

## 🔄 3. Concept-Map Recap (How Earlier Lessons Connect)

* **Days 1–2 (Foundation):** We learned that an application is a machine that receives inputs, follows a sequence of steps, and produces an output.
* **Days 3–4 (Data & Conditions):** We discovered that steps operate on typed values (text, numbers, booleans) and that conditions ask comparison questions yielding strictly `True` or `False`.
* **Days 5–6 (Logic & Decisions):** We learned that `If()` uses that verdict to choose an action fork, and `AND` / `OR` / `NOT` combine several conditions into compound safety gates.
* **TODAY (Day 7 - Phase 3 Begins):** We take a giant step back to look at the whole picture. **Where do those data fields, questions, and decisions come from in the first place?** They come from a **Real Business Problem**! If you don't understand the real human bottleneck, you will build the wrong fields, ask the wrong questions, and write formulas that solve nothing!

## 🧠 4. Prerequisite Active Recall Questions (With Oral Answers)

1. **Question 1:** In Day 6, why did we need compound conditions (`&&`, `||`) instead of a single question?  
   * **Learner Answer:** Because real-world business and safety rules depend on multiple interdependent variables (e.g. pressure AND coolant, or smoke OR gas leak).
2. **Question 2:** In Day 3, why was it critical to decide whether data was a *Number*, *Text*, or *Boolean*?  
   * **Learner Answer:** Because apps store them differently in memory; mathematical calculations and threshold comparisons require numbers (`Value()`), while names require text strings.
3. **Question 3:** What is the fundamental difference between an `Input` and an `Action`?  
   * **Learner Answer:** An `Input` is raw information entered by a human (e.g., machine number or issue notes); an `Action` is work executed by the app (e.g., sending a notification, locking a button, updating storage).

## 💥 5. Today's "WHY THIS EXISTS" Problem (Workplace DX Case Study)

### The Incident: The Clipboard Graveyard & The $32,000 Hydraulic Freeze at Apex Plastics

* **The Scene:** Apex Plastics Extruder Hall #4, operating 24/7 with 12 hydraulic extrusion presses.
* **The Legacy Practice:** Maintenance requests were recorded using a ballpoint pen on paper clipboards hung next to each machine.
* **The Breakdown:**
  1. At 01:15 AM, night shift operator Dara noticed hydraulic pump #3 on Line 4 was leaking fluid and making a high-pitched whine.
  2. Dara wrote on the clipboard: *"Pump 3 leaking fluid, sounds bad."*
  3. During shift change at 06:00 AM, coolant mist and oil overspray soaked the paper clipboard. The technician coming on duty couldn't read the smudged writing.
  4. The clipboard hung on the rail for **14 hours** while the leak worsened.
  5. At 03:30 PM, the hydraulic reservoir ran completely dry. The pump seized solid, the cylinder froze midway through a 200-ton stroke, and the hydraulic line burst, dumping 400 liters of hot oil onto the factory floor.
* **The Financial Impact:**
  * Clean-up and environmental Hazmat disposal: **$8,000**
  * Emergency pump replacement & expedited freight: **$11,000**
  * 18 hours of lost production on Line 4: **$13,000**
  * **Total Financial Disaster: $32,000!**
* **The Failed "Solution":**  
  The next week, an enthusiastic IT junior maker spent 3 days in Power Apps building an app. But he didn't talk to the operators! He created a **14-screen app with 45 mandatory text boxes**, asking for pump serial numbers, manufacturer codes, and engineering specs.
  Operators on the floor took **12 minutes** to fill out one ticket! Within 4 days, operators abandoned the app completely and went back to sticky notes!
* **The Root Cause:**  
  The app failed because nobody scoped the **Real Problem**! The real problem wasn't a lack of technical specs; the real problem was **getting an emergency maintenance alert to an on-duty technician in under 60 seconds with 4 simple taps**!

## 💡 6. Plain-Language Concept Before Technical Vocabulary

* **What is an "App Opportunity"?**  
  An App Opportunity is a specific, repetitive, broken human activity that can be dramatically improved by replacing lost paper, slow emails, or chaotic phone calls with a streamlined, structured tool on a phone, tablet, or PC.
* **The 3 Golden Tests of a True App Opportunity:**
  1. **Repetitive Pain:** Does this happen daily or weekly, causing delays or errors?
  2. **Clear Hand-off:** Does person A collect information that person B must act upon?
  3. **Rules & Visibility:** Does management need to know what happened, when, and whether it was resolved?
* **What is NOT an App Opportunity?**
  * A one-time problem (e.g., choosing a venue for the annual company party ➔ use a quick chat or poll).
  * A static bulletin (e.g., the cafeteria menu for the week ➔ use a PDF or signboard).
  * A policy problem (e.g., workers arriving late ➔ management coaching, not an app).

## 🩺 7. Tangible Everyday Analogies

### Analogy 1: The Doctor's Diagnosis Before Surgery
Imagine walking into a hospital and the surgeon immediately rushes you into an operating room, picks up a scalpel, and starts cutting into your abdomen!  
You would scream: *"Wait! What are you doing? You didn't even ask where it hurts!"*  
A professional doctor always follows a disciplined sequence:
1. Listens to symptoms (*Current Pain*).
2. Measures vitals and orders tests (*Information/Data*).
3. Identifies the underlying illness (*Diagnosis*).
4. Determines treatment criteria (*Rules*).
5. Only then prescribes medicine or performs surgery (*Action/Solution*).  
*Building an app before diagnosing the business problem is performing surgery on the wrong organ!*

### Analogy 2: The Architect's Blueprint Before Laying Bricks
You would never order 10,000 bricks, dump them in a dirt field, and tell bricklayers to start stacking walls without a blueprint! Who is living in the house? How many bedrooms? Where are the water pipes and electrical cables?  
In Power Apps, screens, buttons, and galleries are your bricks. Scoping the problem is your architectural blueprint!

## ⚠️ 12. Four Common Beginner Misconceptions & Corrections

1. **Misconception 1: "Power Apps is for building any idea I have in my head."**  
   * **Trainer Correction:** An app without a business problem is just wasted time. Always start with: *What is currently broken, how much does it cost the company, and who will use this every day?*
2. **Misconception 2: "More input fields make an app more professional."**  
   * **Trainer Correction:** The exact opposite! Every unnecessary field increases the chance that a factory worker will abandon your app. Capture the absolute minimum data needed to trigger the required action.
3. **Misconception 3: "I should start by designing the prettiest screen first."**  
   * **Trainer Correction:** Never design a screen before knowing the data and users. Design flows from *Problem ➔ Process ➔ Data ➔ Screen*, never backwards!
4. **Misconception 4: "If a process is broken on paper, putting it in an app will fix it."**  
   * **Trainer Correction:** Automating a broken process just gives you an automated disaster! If workers don't know who is responsible for fixing a machine, an app won't tell them. Fix the human responsibility first, then build the app to support it.

## ❓ 13. Five Knowledge-Check Questions with Detailed Rationales

1. **Question 1:** What are the 5 parts of the App Opportunity Framework?  
   * **Answer:** Current Problem, Desired Result, People, Information, Rules.
2. **Question 2 (Why question):** *Why* did the junior developer's 14-screen maintenance app at Apex Plastics fail completely, even though it was technically bug-free?  
   * **Answer:** Because he failed to understand the user and context. Floor operators wearing heavy gloves working on hot lines cannot fill out 45 technical text boxes. The app solved the developer's curiosity, not the operator's bottleneck!
3. **Question 3 (What happens if question):** *What happens if* you build a Power App before identifying who the *Actor/Worker* is?  
   * **Answer:** The app will capture data into a database, but no one will be notified or assigned to take action. The tickets will sit in digital storage just like paper sat on the clipboard!
4. **Question 4 (What happens if question):** *What happens if* a process is only performed once every two years? Should you build a custom Power App for it?  
   * **Answer:** Generally NO. Custom apps provide high return on investment (ROI) for repetitive, high-frequency, or high-risk processes. For a once-in-two-years task, standard documentation or an email template is much more cost-effective.
5. **Question 5:** Which user role should be consulted FIRST when scoping an app: the executive director or the front-line worker doing the task?  
   * **Answer:** The front-line worker! While executives set goals, the front-line worker knows the real friction, missing data, and workplace reality.

## 🏆 14. Capstone Independent Practice Challenge: Apex Tooling Die Changeover

### Scenario:
Apex Plastics loses 35 minutes on every mold changeover because operators write die change requests on a whiteboard at the end of the hall. Tooling technicians in the die shop 200 meters away don't see the whiteboard until they walk over for their coffee break.

### Student Deliverable:
Fill out the **5-Part Opportunity Blueprint**:
1. **Current Problem:** Vague whiteboard requests; toolmakers don't know a machine is waiting for a mold change.
2. **Desired Result:** Mold change notification arrives on toolroom tablet the second the previous run finishes.
3. **People:** Line Operator (Logs mold completion), Tooling Tech (Preps & installs new mold), Production Planner (Monitors downtime).
4. **Information:** Machine #, Current Mold #, Next Mold Part #, Priority Level, Timestamp.
5. **Rules:** If Next Mold is "Ready in Rack" ➔ Notify Tech immediately; If "In Cleaning" ➔ Alert Die Wash Station first.

### Evaluation Rubric (4 Performance Levels):
* **Level 4: Master (100%):** All 5 sections clearly completed with realistic operational details, realistic roles, concise data fields, and actionable business rules.
* **Level 3: Proficient (75–99%):** 4 sections complete; minor omission in business rules or roles.
* **Level 2: Developing (50–74%):** Confounded problem with solution; too many unnecessary data fields.
* **Level 1: Novice (<50%):** Jumped straight to describing screen buttons without analyzing problem or stakeholders.

## 🎒 15. 15-Minute Daily Homework: "The Workplace Pain Hunter"

* **Time Required:** $\le 15\text{ minutes}$.
* **Task:**
  1. Identify **ONE real bottleneck or repetitive friction point** at your workplace or home (e.g. tracking who borrowed a tool, ordering printer paper, requesting time off, logging vehicle mileage).

     * *Rules:* What is one rule that guides the decision?
