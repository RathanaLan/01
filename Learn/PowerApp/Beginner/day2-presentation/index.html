Why This Exists

The $18,000 Premature Startup Incident

What happens when workplace steps are executed out of sequence.

⚠

The Out-of-Order Disaster (Apex Plastics)

  • Step A (Mistakenly Done First): Operator pressed green [START] button.
  • Step B (Skipped): Hydraulic cooling pump was NOT turned on.
  • Step C (Skipped): Oil pressure was NOT checked (at 0 PSI).
  • Catastrophe: Pump ran dry, seized metal-to-metal, $18,000 repair bill, 48-hour plant shutdown!
✔

The Proper Sequential Standard (Safe)

  • Step 1: Inspect optical safety light curtain.
  • Step 2: Turn on Master Power & cooling fans.
  • Step 3: Verify hydraulic oil pressure reaches steady 50 PSI.
  • Step 4: Press green [START] button ➔ Safe, smooth operation!

💡 Core Takeaway: In apps and machines, doing the right thing in the wrong order equals total failure!

Key Vocabulary

Concept 1: What is an "Instruction"?

A single, unambiguous command telling a machine to do ONE action.

EVERYDAY ANALOGY 🤖

The Literal Robot

A robot has no intuition. If you tell it "Clean the room", it does not move. You must tell it: "Pick up the box".

➔
WORKPLACE ANALOGY 🔧

The Factory Work Standard

Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs) are made of instructions: "Tighten bolt clockwise to 25 Nm".

➔
POWER APPS ANALOGY 📱

The App Action

In Power Apps, a button has instructions: "Save the data" or "Navigate to Screen 2".

💡 Rule of Instructions: A computer never guesses your mood or intent. You must give it clear, simple, single commands!
Precision in Computing

Clear vs. Vague Instructions

Computers fail when commands are vague. They thrive when commands are exact.

❌

Vague Instructions (Computers Fail)

  • ✖ "Make it work." (Which machine? What outcome?)
  • ✖ "Check the temperature." (Where? What is the safe limit?)
  • ✖ "Tell the boss if it breaks." (Who is the boss? By email or phone?)
  • ✖ "Save the information." (Where? Which fields?)
✔

Exact Instructions (Computers Succeed)

  • ✔ "Read Temperature Sensor #3." (Exact location)
  • ✔ "If temperature > 80°C, turn Red Warning Light ON." (Clear rule)
  • ✔ "Send email notification to Manager with Machine ID." (Exact target)
  • ✔ "Save Operator Name and Date to SharePoint List." (Exact storage)
Core Concept

Concept 2: What is "Sequence"?

The strict, step-by-step order from START to FINISH.

ANCHOR 1 🚩

START

The initial state or trigger before any work begins (e.g. Operator pushes Start button).

➔ORDER
ANCHOR 2 🔄

THE STEPS (1 ➔ 2 ➔ 3)

The exact chain of actions executed one by one, never skipping or scrambling.

➔FINISH
ANCHOR 3 🏁

FINISH

The successful desired outcome when all instructions complete safely.

🧠 Memory Hook: Sequence is like walking up stairs: You cannot step on Step 4 before you step on Step 1, Step 2, and Step 3!

Everyday Analogy

The "Socks Before Shoes" Principle

Two perfect instructions will fail completely if executed in reverse order.

✔

Correct Sequence (Success)

🧦 Socks (ស្រោមជើង) ➔ 👞 Shoes (ស្បែកជើង)
  • Step 1: Put on socks.
  • Step 2: Put on leather work shoes.
  • Result: Comfortable, protected feet ready for a factory shift!
❌

Reversed Sequence (Disaster!)

👞 Shoes (ស្បែកជើង) ➔ 🧦 Socks (ស្រោមជើង)
  • Step 1: Put on heavy leather shoes.
  • Step 2: Try to pull socks over the outside of the shoes!
  • Result: Socks rip, feet unprotected, completely broken!

Lesson for Power Apps: A computer will never stop to ask: 'Are you sure you want to put socks over boots?' It will blindly obey your sequence!

Demonstration 1

The Kitchen Recipe Disaster

A recipe is simply software written for human chefs.

🍰

Correct Recipe Sequence

  • Step 1: Mix flour, eggs, milk, and sugar into smooth batter.
  • Step 2: Pour batter evenly into a greased cake pan.
  • Step 3: Place pan in 180°C oven for 30 minutes.
  • Result: Delicious, golden-brown cake ready to enjoy!
🔥

Scrambled Sequence (Recipe Disaster)

  • Step 1: Put empty metal pan into 180°C oven for 30 minutes.
  • Step 2: Pull out scalding empty pan.
  • Step 3: Pour raw eggs and dry flour onto the dining table.
  • Result: Kitchen fire, ruined pan, messy flour, no food!

Why DX Matters

What Happens When Sequence is Violated?

Digital Transformation (DX) prevents human error by enforcing sequence in software.

⚠

The Old Paper / Memory Trap

  • Operator is tired, rushed, or untrained.
  • Operator skips Step 1 (Safety check) and presses START.
  • Machine jams, motor burns out, $30,000 lost production.
  • Paper clipboard checklist filled out after the shift ended.
📱

The Power Apps Digital Solution

  • Power App locks the [START] button until Step 1 is tapped.
  • App verifies oil pressure sensor reading automatically.
  • Green light appears ONLY when all safety steps are verified in order.
  • Zero mistakes, 100% compliance, safe workers!

Trainer Guidance

4 Common Beginner Mistakes & Fixes

Recognize these classic pitfalls and apply simple corrective habits.

⚠

The Beginner Mistake

  • 1. Skipping Steps: Assuming the app knows to save data before changing screens.
  • 2. Putting Shoes on First: Trying to show a success message before the form is saved.
  • 3. Vague Action Names: Giving buttons unclear names like "Go" or "Click".
  • 4. Leaving No Feedback: Closing the app without telling the operator their report was received.
💡

The Trainer Intervention

  • Fix: Computers cannot read minds. Write every single transition explicitly.
  • Fix: Golden Order: 1. Save data ➔ 2. THEN navigate screens!
  • Fix: Use clear verb-noun labels: "Submit Issue Report".
  • Fix: Always show a green notification banner: "Report #1042 Logged!"
Power Apps Connection

How Power Apps Chains Steps: The Semicolon ( ; )

In Power Fx, the semicolon is simply the code word for "AND THEN".

STEP 1 🗑

Reset(txtIssue)

First, clear the operator's text input box so it is fresh and clean.

;
"AND THEN"
STEP 2 📢

Notify("Report Sent!")

Second, display a reassuring green notification banner at the top of the screen.

Plain English First: "First clear the form, AND THEN show a success banner."
Power Fx Formula: Reset(txtIssue) ; Notify("Report Sent!", NotificationType.Success)

Formula Anatomy

Deconstructing the Sequential Formula

Every character has a logical, human purpose.

Reset(txtIssue) ; Notify("Report Sent!", NotificationType.Success)

1. The First Action

Reset(txtIssue) tells Power Apps to wipe the text field clean so the operator can type a fresh entry.

; = "AND THEN"

2. The Second Action

Notify(...) displays a green toast banner confirming the report safely reached the server.

10-Minute Challenge: The Machine Leak Incident

Design a clean, unambiguous 4-step reporting sequence on your notepad.

📝 The Workplace Scenario

You are walking past CNC Machine #7. You notice hydraulic fluid dripping onto the walkway, creating a slip hazard. The machine is shaking slightly.

Your Instructions:

  1. Write down Step 1: What immediate safety action must happen first?
  2. Write down Step 2: How does the operator open and authenticate the Power App?
  3. Write down Step 3: What exact machine data or photo is recorded?
  4. Write down Step 4: How does the app finish and notify maintenance?

👁 Evaluation Criteria

  • ✔ Order & Logic: Safety is strictly Step 1.
  • ✔ No Vague Words: Uses clear verbs (Tap, Scan, Submit).
  • ✔ Clear Finish: The operator knows the report reached maintenance.
Exemplar 4-Step Solution:
1. Press Emergency Stop & place caution cone.
2. Open 'Maintenance Support' Power App on tablet.
3. Scan Machine #7 QR barcode & snap photo of leak.
4. Tap [Submit Report] ➔ App alerts Maintenance Lead with Ticket #!
Level 4 • Mastery

Flawless Sequence

Level 3 • Competent

Solid Sequence

Level 2 • Developing

Out of Order

Level 1 • Novice

Unsequenced

Single vague sentence ('Fix the leak') with no ordered steps or start/finish markers.
